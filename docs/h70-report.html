Verdict: NEGATIVE — no new GeoTIFF, no slot spent

Hypothesis: An active fault that steps the range-front base level drives a migrating incision wave up each transverse footwall drainage; in chi space that wave is a trapped PAIR of opposite-polarity steepness steps on the same branch: an upstream steepened crest (step_max > 0) and, exactly 500 m downstream along the same flow path (the committed substrate sig band), the wave's leading edge (step_min < 0). pair_strength = min(crest, base_drop), floored at ln(1.25), blockwise z-scored at 40 km, corroborated across >= 3 independent drainages along a common strike, was hypothesised to mark active fault traces the given Quaternary catalogue omits, with single-flank lithologic steps filtered out by the pairing itself.

Mechanism: Kirby & Whipple (2012) base-level-step -> knickpoint; Perron & Royden (2013) chi normalisation; Wobus et al. (2006) / Berlin & Anderson (2007) knickpoint waves: the profile steepens at the crest while the reach immediately downstream of the wave face remains gentle relative to the unperturbed reach below it, so the wave carries one positive and one negative chi-slope step, pinned to the same branch. Gailleton et al. (2019) segmentation motivates the fixed-window two-segment approximation used by the committed 10 m substrate.

Named non-fault mimic process: A resistant-over-weak dipping lithologic couplet (its two contacts make a paired step as a channel crosses the unit twice). Countered by the radiometric-contact-gradient mask, the low-radioelement high-relief core mask, the >= 3-drainage along-strike alignment rule and 40 km blockwise standardization. Secondary mimics: debris-flow headcuts and fan-head trenches (ephemeral-wash mask ksn_max < 10 m^0.9), playa and closed-basin floors (raised_n >= 50), DEM fill artefacts.
