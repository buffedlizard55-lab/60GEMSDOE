Why “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” happened before, and why it cannot happen again

The official format allows data outside the bounds to be null or NaN, but a naive reader that ignores the nodata tag sees NaN and fails a literal [0,1] range test. All our exports use the all-finite container: 0 (not NaN) outside the footprint, values exactly in {0,1}, float32, EPSG:32611, 3730×3292 at 100 m, and the exact official geotransform (100, 0, 243350, 0, −100, 4508550). The on-disk validator (gems52.gates.format_report) re-checks every one of those properties against data/bridge/example_submission.tif before anything is published, and fails closed.

What “promoted” means here (parallel-run protocol)

A file is promoted only if it (a) beat the current holdout best under the shared hide-and-recover evaluator, (b) passed the uniqueness gate vs every registry raster (ρ ≤ 0.90 and ≤ 70% dot overlap), (c) passed the on-disk validator, and (d) was chosen by the separate selector step within the weekly slot cap. This session produced (b) and (c) but failed (a): the lane is a negative result. Negative results are deliverables.
