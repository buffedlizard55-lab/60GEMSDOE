Executive summary

DrivenData competition 306 — DOE GEMS geothermal exploration. Lane: tectonic geomorphology (χ, normalised channel steepness, knickpoint strike alignment). Run date 2026-10-11. ← download page

Bottom line
This run is a negative. The candidate GeoTIFF is validated, unique and reproducible, but the lane's evidence failed its own pre-registered gate, and that failure is the deliverable: it is worth more than a submission slot spent on a map whose skill against faults outside the catalogue is below a uniform-random map of the same mass.

1. How a submission is made (and how to tell whether it is OK)

  1. On the DrivenData competition site, open the submission page for GEMS — Geothermal Exploration and use its file upload control. The competition site is login-walled; the form was described by the operator and could not be re-verified in this sandbox (no authentication).
  2. Upload the single-band GeoTIFF (.tif, or a .zip containing exactly one GeoTIFF). Required geometry, verified from the competition's own statement: EPSG:32611, 100 m pixels, the same bounds/CRS/geotransform as the training rasters, values float32 in [0,1] (predicted fault probability), and pixels outside the footprint null or NaN.
  3. Give it a unique name and a short note (≤140 characters), e.g. kinematic trace x chi-steepness; 3770px; NEGATIVE first test.
  4. Before spending the slot, check the file three ways: open it with rasterio and confirm band count/dtype/CRS/transform match the sample submission; confirm nanmin >= 0 and nanmax <= 1 on the finite pixels (a prior download in this family was rejected with “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]”, which is exactly the failure that this check prevents); and confirm the file is not a near-copy of an already-scored raster (see §5).

The exact validator output for the candidate file is in run_card.json → raster: all six literal checks pass, including passes_portal_range_test: true.

2. The official metric, and what it means

Organizer-confirmed (competition metric page, fetched 2026-10-11): ground truth g on the 100 m grid, prediction p∈[0,1], kernel k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0) with R = 300 m:

  • TPw = Σg∈G maxx: d(x,g) ≤ R p(x)·k(d(x,g))
  • FPw = Σx: p(x)>0 p(x)·[1 − maxg∈G k(d(x,g))]
  • FNw = Σg∈G [1 − maxx: d(x,g) ≤ R p(x)·k(d(x,g))]
  • DTI = TPw / (TPw + α·FPw + β·FNw + ε), with α = 0.2, β = 0.8. Organizer's worked example: TPw=3.00, FPw=1.89, FNw=2.00 → 0.60.

Because FNw = |G| − TPw exactly (each ground-truth pixel contributes at most 1), the score is a function of three numbers only:

DTI = T / (T + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN), with FP = S − M, FN = |G| − T

where T = matched truth mass, S = your total emitted mass, M = the part of S that lies inside some truth pixel's 300 m kernel, |G| = number of hidden truth pixels. Three consequences govern every submission:

  • Missed faults dominate. Each uncovered ground-truth pixel costs 0.8 while each perfectly covered one adds 1.0 — the net marginal value of converting a miss into a hit is ~0.2·(1−DTI), i.e. about 0.14 at DTI≈0.3.
  • Mass is a tax. Every emitted pixel that is not inside a truth kernel pays 0.2·p. At DTI ≈ 0.28 the tax per unit mass (0.2·DTI ≈ 0.056) is small relative to a hit, so extra mass is worth placing only where the chance of being within 300 m of an unseen fault exceeds ~5.6%.
  • Radius beats precision. Anything within 300 m of a truth pixel earns partial credit; a fault map is scored by proximity, not by pixel overlap. This is why dot maps win here and why "dotted" files dominate the family's leaderboard entries.

Three readings follow, and they are the answer to "why 0.2778":

  1. It is a mass-precision point, not a physical achievement. With S = 37,654 and T ≈ 5.5k, 99.3% of the emitted mass (≈37,376 px) sits outside every hidden fault's 300 m kernel and is pure 0.2-weight penalty. The score is carried by the minority of dots that happen to fall within 300 m of an unseen fault. Nothing in the number identifies which physical idea produced the dots: the sibling repositories' inversion of 13 board-calibrated submissions fit every candidate physical basis with zero weight difference (LOO MAE ≈ 0.020, ρ ≈ 0.94) — the leaderboard cannot distinguish the physics, only the mass/recall trade-off.
  2. Its placement rule was "avoid the catalogue, then spread". The file is a strict subset of a larger dotted map, and the only edit between them was deleting the 100–200 m ring around the known faults — 6,436 cells that earned nothing. That deletion is measurable in the score and is the clearest actionable lever in the whole family's history.
  3. It is far from the metric's optimum at its own recall. Holding T fixed and shrinking the mass to 6,000 px gives DTI ≈ 0.40–0.42. Mass, not recall, is the binding constraint at 37.7k dots. Conversely the board maximum 0.3774 back-solves to T ≈ 7.1–7.7k (54–57% recall) at the same mass, so the top of the board is earning ~40% more matched truth mass, not a different kind of map.

But that skill is a 40% enrichment in T, not a qualitative detector — which is exactly what a 0.28 on a 0.38-max board looks like.

Is something higher achievable? Yes, and the target is quantified: (i) recall — the board's best (0.3774) needs ~54–57% of hidden truths within 300 m, versus the champion's ~39%; that is +0.10 of DTI earned the hard way, by finding faults the catalogue misses; (ii) mass discipline — the same recall at 6,000 px of mass is worth ~+0.13. Nothing in the metric forbids a much higher score: a hypothetical map that covered every hidden fault with no unmatched mass would score near 1.0. The practical ceiling is set by how many of the hidden faults any method can place within 300 m, and the present frontier (0.3774) is nowhere near the metric's limit.

5. Why the verdict is "negative" rather than "promising"

  • The lane's whole claim is localisation: the traces should be where unseen faults are. At matched mass the trace field is worse than random at 8 of 9 budgets, and its published holdout DTI (0.0064) is below the null (0.0081). A detector that is beaten by a coin is not promotable, and the protocol forbids spending a submission slot on it.
  • The pre-registered first test also failed, on both substrates. Its per-fold signs are consistently positive (4/5 and 5/5 folds in the two runs), so the honest summary is "weak positive effect in the expected direction, not established", not "no effect shown".
  • The uniqueness and leakage checks both pass, so the negative is not an artefact of duplication or of a shortcut: the field genuinely carries no information about the off-catalogue truth (and the canary shows it is not secretly reading the labels).
  • The instrument-bias diagnostic says the off-catalogue population used here is anti-correlated with relief. That does not rescue the lane, but it does mean the next experiment must fix the instrument before the hypothesis is declared dead.

6. What must be true before the next submission slot is spent

  1. Instrument first. Re-thin the off-catalogue population within relief deciles (preserve the real fault/relief joint distribution instead of spreading the sample into basins), then re-score every candidate raster in the registry against the corrected instrument. Until the null is credible, no lane's numbers mean anything.
  2. Real 10 m DEM. The free, public 3DEP products are reachable from a GitHub Actions runner (the workflow in this repository probes three of them and commits a receipt); the sandbox itself has no network path to prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com. χ/ksn at 100 m cannot resolve first-order channels, which is the most likely cause of the routing diagnostics above.
  3. A detector that beats the null on the corrected instrument at matched mass, with the pre-registered bench being the best promotable holdout DTI in the family's own record (0.190147 on its folds), before any board slot is used.
  4. Mass discipline. Whatever the detector, the emission policy should be set by the metric's own marginal rule at the measured DTI, and every file should be checked for the 100–200 m ring deletion that the champion's history shows is free money.
