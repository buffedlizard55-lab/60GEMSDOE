1Relief-stratified off-catalogue instrument — fix the measurement before any more geology.

Layers / data nothing new: SGMC quaternary fault raster + the cube's band 12 (detrended elevation) and band 19; the committed 3DEP-derived pack (relief_local, slope_p90, dem_mean) for stratification.

Physical signature none — this is an instrument, not a detector. It replaced an artefact: this run measured that the current thinning puts the off-catalogue sample in low-relief, high-elevation ground (AUC 0.90/0.90/0.88 for relief/slope/elevation), so every relief-based method is handicapped by construction and every "no skill" verdict is suspect.

Why it matters for catalogue-missing faults the catalogue's blind spot is not sampled uniformly: faults missing from USGS/INGENIOUS are disproportionately those with subtle or buried expression, which live in particular relief/landform settings. A prevalence-preserving, relief-stratified sample is the only way to ask "would this map have found a fault the catalogue missed without being told where to look".

Difference from prior work the sibling repositories all thin by maximal separation to a global |G| pin; none stratifies. This is the first instrument change, and it retro-validates or retro-invalidates the whole family's negative ledger (H91/H93/H94/H95/H96).

2χ–ksn knickpoint traces on the real 10 m 3DEP DEM (the lane's mandated substrate).

Layers / data USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second seamless DEM at native 10 m (VRT, public domain); competition band 12 (detrended elevation, used this run) as a cross-check; band 19 (its slope) as a sanity mask. Fetch happens in a GitHub Actions runner; the sandbox has no route to the S3 host.

Physical signature χ (Perron & Royden) and normalised channel steepness ksn (Kirby & Whipple); knickpoints where the local dz/dχ steps up (Gailleton segmentation); the detector is the collinear chain of knickpoints from ≥3 independent first-order basins along a common strike — implemented and unit-tested in this repository (src/gems60/surface.py::strike_trace_support).

Why it can catch a fault the catalogue misses the catalogue is a mapping product: it under-records faults with no young surface rupture and faults first recognised by paleoseismology. Those faults still put a step in every channel that crosses them, and the step survives in the χ-space profile even when the surface trace does not. A line of such steps from independent drainages is a fault trace or nothing.

Difference from prior work the family's 1 m lidar work (r7-nms3-dem10-scarp, H20) detects scarps; its H43 lane ran knickpoints on the 100 m detrended-elevation proxy with strict D8 and died in flats (max accumulation 109 px). This lane uses real elevation, priority-flood + ε routing, the χ transform, and cross-drainage strike collinearity; this run is the first time the routing has been diagnosable (it is not yet physical at 100 m: 31% of cells raised by filling, per-cell A-vs-L exponent ≈0.03).

3Emission hygiene: catalogue-ring deletion, playa/ephemeral masking, and the metric's own marginal-rule budget.

Layers / data labels.tif (catalogue) for the ring; the 3DEP pack's relief_local/steep_frac and the drainage network's own accumulation for playa and ephemeral-channel masking; the metric algebra for the budget.

Physical signature not geology — base levels. Playa floors and closed basins have no through-going channel, so any "knickpoint" there is a base-level artefact; ephemeral channels with very large contributing-area thresholds carry no steady-state χ signal.

Why it matters for catalogue-missing faults it converts emission mass into expected credit: at DTI ≈ 0.28 the metric accepts a dot only if its chance of lying within 300 m of an unseen fault exceeds ~5.6%, and the champion's own history shows the 100–200 m ring around known faults earns exactly zero while paying the 0.2 mass tax.

Difference from prior work the family's dot maps apply a 200 m ring and a global budget; none of the reviewed lanes ties the budget to a measured holdout DTI, and none uses a drainage-network-derived playa mask. Both are implemented here (`nodes.budget_from_marginal_rule`, `surface.build` confounds).

4χ-wave discrimination: separate base-level-fall waves from fault steps.

Layers / data 10 m DEM + derived drainage; the competition cube's band 12 only as a fallback. Key auxiliary: the elevation of the nearest base level (playa edge / trunk junction) for each first-order basin.

Physical signature a knickpoint generated by a falling base level propagates upstream and its χ position is set by the wave's age and the basin's drainage area; a fault step is fixed in space and independent of the basin's size. Multiple knickpoints in one basin at χ positions predicted by a single wave time, versus multiple basins sharing one spatial line, separate the two.

Why it matters for catalogue-missing faults the named mimic process for hypothesis 2 is exactly the transient wave; without this test a "trace" of knickpoints can be a wavefront crossing a structural grain, which would produce a believable but wrong map.

Difference from prior work the family has never modelled χ-space wave timing (H43b used per-basin knickpoint strength only, with no χ-age test).

5Multi-physics collinearity: require a magnetic / gravity / conductivity step along the same topographic trace.

Layers / data cube bands 14–16 (RTP magnetic anomaly, TMI, vertical and horizontal TMI slopes), band 17 (top-of-crustal magnetic source depth), bands 8–9 (isostatic gravity anomaly and slope), bands 1–2 (surface conductivity, depth-to-conductive-base); USGS GeoDAWN Th/K, U/K, U/Th and TMI extensions as independent confirmations.

Physical signature a fault that offsets magnetised or conductive rock produces a lateral step in those fields, running along the same azimuth as the topographic trace. Requiring the step to be collinear with the geomorphic trace removes lithologic contacts (which are usually also conductivity anomalies, but lack a coincident magnetic step when offset is small).

Why it matters for catalogue-missing faults buried faults with no surface rupture still offset magnetic basement and conductivity structure; this is the one channel that can see a fault the topography cannot, and the cube already contains these layers.

Difference from prior work the family's H16/H18/H25 lanes used these layers through ridge/fusion templates and gradients, but never gated a geomorphic trace on a coincident collinear step; this lane's output is a line, so the coincidence test is well posed.

Every "expected ΔDTI" above is an estimate from the metric algebra with the published pins, not a measurement. The only measurements in this repository are labelled HOLDOUT-DTI or PUBLIC-BOARD on the other pages.
