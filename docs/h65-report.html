What the result means. At 100 m the same lane passed E1 (H60-A: +0.1089 [0.0586, 0.1569]). At 10 m it does not, and its HOLDOUT-DTI drops from 0.01639 to 0.01158. So resolution was not what held the lane back. The 100 m "signal" was most likely smoothing of range-front slope breaks, not channel knickpoints. A descriptive check (evidence/h65_diagnostics.json) agrees: - Within 300 m of the catalogue, 10 m steps ≥ ln 2 are only 1.19× over-represented relative to the footprint, and knickpoints only 1.04×. - Step-bearing channels sit lower in the local relief (median −16 m against a 20 km median) than the catalogue (+7 m).

The mimicking non-fault processes dominate: lithologic contacts, transient knickpoints that have migrated away from their source, channel-head transitions near A_ch, and playa or pluvial base levels. Do not rerun the knickpoint lane at any resolution.

Why the group's 0.2778 champion scores what it does, and whether we can beat it. The official metric is verified on page 967: a distance-weighted Tversky index, α = 0.2 (FP), β = 0.8 (FN), triangular kernel with R = 300 m. With β four times α, a sparse dot that lands within 300 m of a hidden fault earns credit on several truth pixels and pays at most 0.2 of its own mass. The champion (h33-2-b2, re-measured this session) is built that way: - 37,654 binary dots, 0 NaN; - no dot closer than 223.6 m to a mapped fault; median distance 1,965 m; only 5.8 % within 300 m.

So it spends its whole budget off the catalogue, where the hidden faults are (the test faults are by definition not in the USGS database), and its 0.2708 → 0.2778 gain came from deleting catalogue-adjacent mass that could never earn credit. To beat it we need new information about where unmapped faults are. Local cleanup is not enough: the public top is now 0.4133 (leaderboard, checked 2026-10-11), which is +49 % over 0.2778. Every knickpoint-lane run in this repository that reached a holdout comparison scored at or below a mass-matched random control: the chi-cast lane (0.00039 vs 0.00042), GEMS61 (paired difference vs random −0.0375), H60-A (0.01639 vs 0.03908) and H65-1 (0.01158 vs 0.03908). The first run stopped at its first test (AUC 0.224). So this lane cannot provide that information.

New hypotheses (ranked; registry/hypotheses.json H65-2 to H65-5; none validated, because the 3-experiment budget was used up): 1. H65-2, mountain-front piedmont junction at 10 m, weighted by front sinuosity (Bull & McFadden 1977). Range-bounding faults sit at the hillslope/fan slope break even where scarps are buried. It reuses the committed 10 m runner, so the cost is one new band plus about 4 min on a runner. It is the first experiment for the next session, and it must pass the blocked holdout before any slot is used. 2. H65-3, fan-apex alignment plus fan-head trenching. Uses channel confinement, not steepness. 3. H65-4, divide asymmetry of tilted blocks (χ across divides: Willett et al. 2014; Forte & Whipple 2018). 4. H65-5, knickpoint recurrence in χ (Crosby & Whipple 2006). Ranked low because of this session's 1.04× support enrichment.

Free official data for all four: USGS 3DEP 10 m (3DEP; tiles at https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/13/TIFF/current/). It is public domain, and access was verified this session from the runner.
