Science, hypotheses and verified sources

Hypothesis H60-A

Bedrock channels that cross an active normal fault carry an upstream-steepening knickpoint; a knickpoint corroborated by >=3 independent drainage networks inside a strike-aligned corridor marks a fault trace, including traces absent from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue.

Mechanism. Fault-crossing channels record a step in rock-uplift rate as a step in normalized steepness ksn = S*(A/A0)^theta along chi (Kirby & Whipple 2012; Perron & Royden 2013). Single-channel knickpoints are ambiguous; strike-aligned corroboration across drainage divides is not, because no single-channel process is strike-aligned.

Named non-fault mimic. Lithologic contact / basin-fill boundary: a hard-to-soft rock contact also steps ksn and can be strike-aligned across drainages. Secondary mimics: closed-basin (playa) base level, ephemeral/alluvial reaches, regional climate-precipitation gradient, and post-wildfire or landslide-derived steepening.

Confound controls.

  • climate/regional gradient: knickpoint magnitude is z-scored inside 40 km blocks (blockwise_zscore), never ranked globally
  • playa / closed-basin base level: cells whose priority-flood raise exceeds 20 m are excluded (542,309 px measured)
  • lithology / basin fill: cells with depth_to_base_surf above its 90th footprint percentile are excluded (thick sedimentary cover = no bedrock channel)
  • ephemeral / alluvial: cells with cond_surf above its 95th percentile are excluded (clay-rich, evaporitic, non-bedrock)
  • channel-hood: only pixels with contributing area >= 20 cells (0.20 km^2) are eligible
