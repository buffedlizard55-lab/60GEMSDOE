Hypothesis, mechanism, mimic

Hypothesis

Channels crossing a withheld fault segment carry a larger chi-normalised steepness step (relative ksn step) than random lines of the same orientation, once the lane brief's four confounds (playa base levels, ephemeral washes, lithology, climate gradient) are masked; and knickpoints corroborated by >= 3 independent first-order drainages along a common strike therefore locate faults the catalogue omits.

Mechanism

Stream power: ksn = (U/K)^(1/n), so a step in rock uplift rate U across an active normal fault produces a RELATIVE (ratio) step in ksn at the crossing and upstream of it (Kirby & Whipple 2012, doi:10.1016/j.jsg.2012.07.009). The chi transform puts channels of different drainage area on one axis so those ratios are comparable between drainages (Perron & Royden 2013, doi:10.1002/esp.3302); the committed 10 m reduction segments the chi-z profile per Gailleton et al. (2019), doi:10.5194/esurf-7-211-2019.

The named non-fault process that mimics it

Steepness LEVEL, not steepness STEP. Mapped faults sit preferentially on range fronts, where channels are steep. Any statistic carrying the ksn level (absolute dksn, piedmont curvature, slope) inherits that. MEASURED: a statistic containing no step information at all - block-centred log(ksn) - scores +0.1946 [+0.1695, +0.2195] on the same first test (n=1823), which is larger than half the secondary arm's effect. Dipping resistant-over-weak lithology, debris-flow reaches and orographic discharge gradients are the other named mimics; the first two are masked, the third is block-detrended.
