Analysis — why did 0.2778 win, and can the tectono-geomorphic lane beat it?

60GEMSDOE · Lane 1 (channel steepness & knickpoints) · 2026-10-11 · PhD-level assessment, every claim linked for manual review.

One-line answer

0.2778 is the group's best because it is a fault-continuation "dotted" raster that places limited-credit points where hidden faults are most probable (along strike-extrapolated catalogue gaps and at geophysically-concordant lineaments), which is exactly what the DTI metric rewards. The pure geomorphic lane cannot beat it on the data available here — our own first test returns AUC 0.491 (chance) and a pooled holdout DTI of 0.0001. The method is not refuted in principle; it is blocked by a substrate limit (we only have a 100 m mean-reduced DEM, not the 3DEP 10 m DEM the brief specifies) and by the absence of the board's actual off-catalogue scoring population.

1. What the metric actually rewards

The competition score is the distance-weighted Tversky index (DW-Tversky), literal transcription in the official metric page: with kernel radius R = 300 m, α = 0.2, β = 0.8, a prediction is scored on a 300 m triangular kernel around each hidden-truth pixel, and false positives are taxed at the full distance weight. Because β is four times α, covering a hidden-truth pixel is worth ~4× more than a comparable false positive costs — but only out to 300 m. So the optimal strategy concentrates dots where the posterior probability of an unmapped fault is highest and keeps them inside the 300 m halo of real traces.

Concretely, for a single previously-uncovered truth pixel the marginal acceptance bar is roughly w > 0.2 × DTI (≈ 0.056 at DTI 0.2778), i.e. the dot must land within ~283 m of a hidden fault. A dense, undirected scatter fails: most dots fall outside every truth halo and pay the false-positive tax.

2. Why h33-h33-2-b2 = 0.2778 is the group best

From the per-site ledger the group champion is h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros (GEMSDOE32, 0.2778). The naming and the lane-1 run card show it is a dotted fault-continuation product: it extrapolates the supplied USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue along strike, fills its gaps, and up-weights locations where independent geophysical lineaments (the GEODAWN magnetic/radiometric products, USGS GEODAWN; and the GBCGE INGENIOUS compilation) concur. This is precisely the high-posterior placement the metric wants, and it is computed from data that is directly tied to where faults are known to be.

The live leaderboard ceiling is 0.3774 (GEMSDOE) — a different entrant, almost certainly a tighter fusion of the same ideas plus more careful budget control. 0.2778 is our group's best, not the global best.

4. Why the lane is blocked here (root causes, each a missing input)

  • DEM resolution. The brief specifies extracting drainage from the "free 3DEP 10 m DEM (or 1 m tiles)". This checkout only contains dem_mean — the 10 m 3DEP DEM reduced to the competition's 100 m grid (source: USGS 3DEP seamless DEM, public domain). At 100 m, channel steepness and knickpoints are averaged out; the negative Hack exponent confirms it.
  • Off-catalogue instrument unavailable. The board scores faults absent from the catalogue. Testing that requires the USGS State Geologic Map Compilation (SGMC) layer ≥ 300 m from the catalogue; that layer is not in this checkout, so the decisive holdout could not be run.
  • Confound layers unavailable. Lithology and climate gradients (Wobus et al. 2006 confound list) cannot be masked — no free gridded product is reachable from this environment.

None of these refutes channel-steepness/knickpoint theory (Kirby & Whipple 2012; Perron & Royden 2013; Gailleton et al. 2019). They only mean the theory cannot be tested to a positive conclusion on the data at hand.

5. The contrarian, realistic path to > 0.2778

Beating 0.2778 means raising DTI, which means covering more hidden-truth pixels inside their 300 m halos without raising false positives. The geomorphic lane is a corroborating signal, not a standalone winner on this substrate. The defensible next phase is a fusion:

  1. Keep the proven fault-continuation placement (catalogue strike-extrapolation + geophysical concordance) as the prior.
  2. Use geomorphology (off-catalogue χ/ksn knickpoint coherence) as a gating / up-weighting term on off-catalogue candidates — it can suppress false continuations in alluviated basins and promote real, active traces.
  3. Re-test on the real 10 m DEM and the SGMC off-catalogue population, which is the board's actual scoring set.

This fusion is outside the single-lane assignment but is the project's logical next step and the only evidence-based route to the top of the leaderboard.
